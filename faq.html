FAQ and Glossary

Short answers first, then the vocabulary.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

Frequently asked questions

What CRS score do I need?

There is no fixed pass mark. Each round has its own cut-off. In 2026, before the November rule change, Canadian Experience Class rounds ranged from 507 to 523, French-language rounds from 382 to 435, and healthcare, trades and transport rounds from 467 to 477. See the draw tracker for current figures.

Can I immigrate without a job offer?

Yes. Express Entry does not require a job offer, and most invited candidates do not have one. Since 3 November 2026 a job offer in a listed high-wage occupation adds 50 to 100 points; other offers add none.

How much money do I need?

For Express Entry, budget $1,590 per adult in government fees, plus biometrics, tests and medicals. Federal Skilled Worker and Skilled Trades applicants must also show settlement funds from $15,263 for one person. Use the cost calculator.

Is there an age limit?

No program has an upper age limit, but CRS age points peak from 20 to 29 and reach zero at 45. Older applicants usually rely on language, a provincial nomination, Canadian experience or family sponsorship.

How long does Express Entry take?

You can enter the pool as soon as you have language results and a credential assessment. After an invitation you have 60 days to apply, and IRCC aims to process most applications in about six months. Time waiting in the pool depends on your score.

Does my partner have to take a language test?

Only if you want the spouse points (up to 20 for language). In some cases the higher-scoring partner should be the principal applicant; run the calculator both ways.

Can I include my parents in my application?

No. Economic applications cover you, your spouse or partner and dependent children. Parents come later through sponsorship or a super visa. See family sponsorship.

Do I need a consultant or lawyer?

No. If you hire one, verify their licence first. See avoid fraud.

Is this site connected to the government?

No. We are an independent publisher. Applications are made only through canada.ca.

Glossary

AIPAtlantic Immigration Program. Employer-driven permanent residence for the four Atlantic provinces.
CECCanadian Experience Class. Express Entry program for people with a year of skilled work in Canada.
CLB / NCLCCanadian Language Benchmarks (English) and Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens (French). The 1 to 12 scale used for language requirements.
CRSComprehensive Ranking System. The points system that ranks Express Entry profiles.
DLIDesignated learning institution. A school approved to host international students.
ECAEducational credential assessment. Confirms that a foreign credential is valid and equal to a Canadian one.
FSW / FSTFederal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs, both managed through Express Entry.
IECInternational Experience Canada. Youth mobility work permits, including Working Holiday.
IRCCImmigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada, the federal department that decides applications.
ITAInvitation to apply. Issued to Express Entry candidates selected in a round; valid for 60 days.
LMI factorLabour Market Integration factor. CRS section added on 3 November 2026 covering Canadian work experience, high-wage experience and high-wage job offers.
LMIALabour Market Impact Assessment. Employer application showing a foreign hire will not harm the labour market.
NOC / TEERNational Occupational Classification, and its Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities categories 0 to 5. Express Entry covers TEER 0 to 3.
PALProvincial attestation letter required with most study permit applications.
PGWPPost-graduation work permit. Open work permit for eligible graduates.
PNPProvincial Nominee Program. Provinces nominate candidates for permanent residence.
PRPermanent resident or permanent residence.
RCICRegulated Canadian Immigration Consultant, licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants.
RPRFRight of permanent residence fee. $600 per adult since 30 April 2026.
