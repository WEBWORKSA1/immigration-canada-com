Study in Canada

A Canadian credential can lead to work experience and permanent residence, but the route is narrower than it was. New study permits are capped, and the work permit after graduation now depends on what and where you study.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

The 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan sets a target of 150,000 new international student arrivals a year. Places are limited, so the choice of institution and program matters more than before.

What you need for a study permit

  • A letter of acceptance from a designated learning institution (DLI). Only DLIs can host international students.
  • A provincial or territorial attestation letter for most applicants. It confirms your place counts within that province's share of the national cap. Some groups are exempt; the list has changed more than once, so check the current rules.
  • Proof of funds. First-year tuition, travel, and a minimum cost-of-living amount that IRCC adjusts each year. Quebec sets its own amount.
  • For Quebec: a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) before the federal application.
  • A clear study plan. Officers must be satisfied you will leave at the end of your authorized stay; intending to apply for permanent residence later is allowed and is not a contradiction.
  • Biometrics, and a medical exam or police certificate where required.

Working while you study

Eligible full-time students at a DLI can work off campus up to 24 hours a week during academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks, without a separate work permit. Co-op or internship placements that are part of the program need a co-op work permit.

The post-graduation work permit

The post-graduation work permit (PGWP) is an open work permit of up to three years, and it is the bridge to the Canadian Experience Class. Key conditions:

  • The program must be at a PGWP-eligible institution and at least eight months long.
  • You must prove language ability: CLB 7 for university graduates and CLB 5 for college and other programs.
  • Graduates of non-degree programs generally need a field of study linked to an occupation in long-term shortage. Bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates are not subject to the field-of-study rule.
  • You apply within 180 days of completing the program, and you can receive only one PGWP.

From graduate to permanent resident

  1. Work for at least one year in a skilled occupation (TEER 0 to 3) on your PGWP.
  2. Enter the Express Entry pool under the Canadian Experience Class. Canadian study adds 15 or 30 CRS points and one year of Canadian work adds 35 to 40, with up to 70 more for high-wage occupations since November 2026.
  3. Consider your province's graduate stream. Several provinces nominate graduates of their own institutions.

Model your score as a future graduate before committing to a program; the difference between a one-year certificate and a degree can decide the outcome.

Choosing a program without being misled

  • Confirm the institution on the official DLI list and check that the specific program is PGWP-eligible.
  • Be careful with agents paid by institutions. Their incentive is enrolment, not your immigration outcome.
  • A letter of acceptance must be verified by the institution with IRCC. Fake letters have led to removals.
