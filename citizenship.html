Canadian Citizenship

Permanent residents can apply for citizenship once they have lived in Canada long enough and met a short list of conditions. Some people born abroad are already citizens through a Canadian parent.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

Requirements for permanent residents

  1. Physical presence. At least 1,095 days in Canada during the five years before you apply. Days spent in Canada as a temporary resident or protected person before becoming a permanent resident count as half days, up to a maximum credit of 365 days.
  2. Income tax. You filed taxes, if required to, for at least three years within that five-year period.
  3. Language. Applicants aged 18 to 54 must show CLB 4 or higher in speaking and listening, in English or French.
  4. Citizenship test. Applicants aged 18 to 54 take a 20-question test on rights, responsibilities, history, geography, government and symbols. The pass mark is 15 correct answers.
  5. Status. You are a permanent resident with no unfulfilled conditions, not under a removal order and not barred for criminal or security reasons.

Apply with a buffer. IRCC counts days up to the date you sign the application, and a miscounted trip can leave you short. Many applicants wait until they have a few weeks more than 1,095 days.

Fees

Adults pay a processing fee plus the right of citizenship fee, which is indexed each year and has been $123 since 31 March 2026. Minors pay a lower fee and no right of citizenship fee. Check the IRCC fee list for the current total before paying.

The process

  1. Calculate your physical presence with the official online calculator and keep the printout.
  2. Apply online with identity documents, language proof and the fee.
  3. Receive an acknowledgement of receipt, then an invitation to the test.
  4. Attend an interview if asked.
  5. Take the oath at a citizenship ceremony. You are a citizen from that moment and can apply for a passport.

Citizenship by descent

A child born outside Canada to a Canadian parent is usually a citizen from birth and applies for a citizenship certificate as proof, not for a grant of citizenship. Under changes to the Citizenship Act that took effect in December 2025, citizenship by descent is no longer limited to the first generation born abroad in many cases: people born before the change who were excluded by the old first-generation limit may now be citizens, and for children born abroad after it, a Canadian parent who was also born abroad can pass on citizenship if that parent spent at least 1,095 days in Canada before the birth.

If a parent or grandparent was Canadian, check this route first. It is faster and cheaper than any immigration program and comes with no residency obligation.

Dual citizenship

Canada allows you to hold another citizenship. Whether you can keep your original one depends on the law of that country, so check before taking the oath.
