Avoid Immigration Fraud

People planning a move to Canada are targeted by fake job offers, unlicensed advisers and look-alike websites. A few checks stop almost all of it.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

About this website: immigration-canada.com is an independent publisher. We are not the Government of Canada, we do not process applications, and we will never ask you to pay a government fee to us. Official applications happen only on canada.ca.

Seven signs of a scam

  1. A guarantee. No one can guarantee a visa, a job, a nomination or faster processing. Only IRCC or a province decides.
  2. Payment for a job offer. Genuine employers do not sell jobs or charge workers for a Labour Market Impact Assessment.
  3. Unusual payment methods. Gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers to an individual, or pressure to pay today.
  4. Contact from a free email account claiming to be a government officer. IRCC writes from addresses ending in canada.ca or gc.ca and does not threaten arrest or deportation by phone.
  5. A website that imitates the government. Official federal pages are on canada.ca or a gc.ca address. Forms and guides there are free.
  6. Advice to lie. Misrepresentation on an application leads to refusal and a five-year ban, and you are responsible even if an adviser filled in the form.
  7. An adviser who will not put their name on the application. Paid representatives must be declared on the Use of a Representative form.

Who is allowed to charge for immigration advice

In Canada only these people can be paid to advise or represent you on an immigration matter:

  • Lawyers and paralegals in good standing with a provincial or territorial law society.
  • Notaries who are members of the Chambre des notaires du Québec.
  • Consultants licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (titles RCIC and RISIA).

How to verify in two minutes

  • Consultants: search the name or licence number in the College's public register and confirm the status is active.
  • Lawyers: search the directory of the law society for the province where they practise.
  • Ask for a written retainer agreement that lists services and fees before paying.

Education agents abroad and recruiters are not authorised to give immigration advice unless they hold one of those licences.

Protect your documents and accounts

  • Create your own IRCC account with your own email and keep the password. Do not let an adviser be the only person with access.
  • Ask for copies of everything submitted in your name.
  • Send passport scans only when a step in a real application needs them.

If something has gone wrong

  • Report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
  • Report immigration-specific fraud through the IRCC fraud reporting page or the Canada Border Services Agency tip line.
  • Complain about a licensed consultant to the College, or about a lawyer to their law society.
  • If false information was submitted for you, get advice from a licensed professional quickly; correcting the record early is better than waiting.
