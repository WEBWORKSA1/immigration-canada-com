Immigration Levels Plan 2027–2029

Each autumn the federal government sets how many people it plans to admit over the next three years. The plan released on 5 November 2026 holds the overall numbers steady and shifts the mix toward provinces and people already in Canada.

Temporary residents

The plan counts new arrivals of workers and students: 220,000 workers a year, made up of 170,000 under the International Mobility Program and 50,000 under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program, and 150,000 international students. Visitors, asylum claimants, permit extensions and applications made from inside Canada are not counted in these figures. The stated aim is to bring the temporary resident population below 5% of the total population.

The one-time 2027 measure

Separate from the 380,000 target, the government plans up to 200,000 additional permanent residence spaces in 2027 for people already living in Canada:

  • Up to 100,000 workers, plus their families. Provinces and territories receive extra nomination spaces, and applicants already in the Atlantic Immigration Program or in programs that have closed to new applications can be processed.
  • Up to 100,000 on humanitarian grounds. This covers remaining applications under the Hong Kong and Ukraine family reunification pathways, people already found eligible on humanitarian and compassionate grounds, and spouses, partners and dependent children already in Canada.

How the worker spaces will be divided among provinces, and the dates, had not been announced when this page was reviewed.

French-speaking immigration

The target share of French-speaking permanent residents settling outside Quebec rises from 9.5% in 2027 to 10.5% in 2028 and 12% in 2029. That target is the main reason French-language Express Entry rounds are large and have comparatively low cut-offs.

What it means if you are planning to apply

  • Express Entry gets tighter. A lower Federal High Skilled target with a pool of more than 228,000 candidates points to continued high cut-offs for general profiles.
  • Provinces matter more. A larger PNP target and extra 2027 nomination room favour candidates with a provincial connection.
  • Being in Canada helps. Both the regular plan and the one-time measure prioritise people already working or living in the country.
  • French is the clearest lever for candidates without Canadian experience.
