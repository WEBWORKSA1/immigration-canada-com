Express Entry

Express Entry is the online system Canada uses to manage applications for permanent residence from skilled workers. It is a ranking system, not a program: you qualify under a program, enter a pool, and are invited if your score is high enough.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

The three programs

ProgramCore requirements
Federal Skilled WorkerOne year of continuous skilled work in the last ten years, CLB 7 in all four language skills, a secondary credential or higher, at least 67 points on the selection grid, and settlement funds.
Canadian Experience ClassOne year of skilled work in Canada in the last three years. CLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 occupations, CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3. No education minimum and no settlement funds.
Federal Skilled TradesTwo years in a skilled trade in the last five years, CLB 5 speaking and listening, CLB 4 reading and writing, and either a full-time job offer of at least a year or a Canadian certificate of qualification.

Part of the Provincial Nominee Program also runs through Express Entry. A nomination from one of those streams adds 600 points.

From profile to permanent residence

  1. Get your documents. A language test less than two years old and, for education outside Canada, an educational credential assessment.
  2. Submit a profile. It is free and stays in the pool for up to 12 months.
  3. Receive a score. The Comprehensive Ranking System scores age, education, language, labour market integration and more. Estimate yours.
  4. Wait for a round. IRCC invites the top-ranked candidates in program-specific, category-based or general rounds. See every round.
  5. Apply within 60 days of an invitation, with police certificates, medical exams, proof of work experience and, where required, proof of funds.
  6. Decision. IRCC aims to process most complete applications in about six months.

Category-based rounds in 2026

Alongside program rounds, IRCC invites candidates who meet a category definition. The current categories are:

  • French-language proficiency (NCLC 7 in all four skills)
  • Healthcare and social services occupations
  • Science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) occupations
  • Trade occupations
  • Education occupations
  • Transport occupations
  • Physicians, senior managers and researchers with Canadian work experience
  • Skilled military recruits

Occupation categories generally require 12 months of experience in a listed occupation within the past three years. For physicians, senior managers and researchers that experience must be in Canada. The exact rules are set in the instructions for each round.

What changed on 3 November 2026

IRCC added a Labour Market Integration factor worth up to 150 points. It rewards either Canadian work experience, with a bonus of 20, 40 or 70 points for at least 12 months in an occupation on the high-wage list, or a valid job offer in a high-wage occupation, worth 50, 70 or 100 points. Job offers no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment. A job offer outside the high-wage list earns no points but may still count toward program eligibility.

Money: fees and funds

Since 30 April 2026 the processing fee is $990 per adult and the right of permanent residence fee is $600 per adult; dependent children are $270 each. Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades applicants must also show settlement funds, starting at $15,263 for one person. Calculate your total.
