Provincial Nominee Programs

Provinces and territories nominate people who match their own labour needs. A nomination is the single largest boost available in Express Entry, and the federal plan gives provinces more room in 2027.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

How nomination works

There are two routes. Enhanced streams are linked to Express Entry: you need an Express Entry profile, and a nomination adds 600 CRS points, which has been enough for an invitation in every Provincial Nominee Program round. Base streams sit outside Express Entry: after nomination you apply to IRCC directly, and processing takes longer. Either way the province nominates and IRCC makes the final decision on permanent residence.

Most streams expect a connection to the province: a job offer, work experience or study there, or an occupation the province is short of. Many use an expression-of-interest system with their own points grid.

Regional programs outside the PNP

Atlantic Immigration Program

An employer-driven federal program for New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador. You need a job offer from a designated employer and a settlement plan. The annual target rises from 4,000 to 6,000 under the 2027–2029 plan.

Rural Community Immigration Program

The pilot covering 14 rural communities is becoming a permanent program, with planned admissions of 5,500 in 2027, 6,000 in 2028 and 6,500 in 2029. Candidates need a job offer from a designated employer in a participating community.

Additional nomination room in 2027

The 2027–2029 plan includes a one-time measure of up to 200,000 extra permanent residence spaces in 2027 for people already in Canada, part of which gives provinces and territories more spaces to nominate workers. Allocations by province had not been published when this page was reviewed.
