Family Sponsorship

Canadian citizens and permanent residents can sponsor certain relatives for permanent residence. Family class has no points test; it rests on the relationship being genuine and the sponsor being eligible.

Who can sponsor

  • You are at least 18 and a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident living in Canada, or a person registered under the Indian Act.
  • Citizens living abroad can sponsor a spouse, partner or child if they will return to Canada when the person lands. Permanent residents must be living in Canada.
  • You are not receiving social assistance for a reason other than disability, and you are not in default on an earlier undertaking, a support order or an immigration loan.

Who you can sponsor

Spouse, common-law or conjugal partner

A spouse is someone you are legally married to. A common-law partner is someone you have lived with in a marriage-like relationship for at least 12 continuous months. A conjugal partner is someone outside Canada you have been in a committed relationship with for at least a year but could not live with or marry because of barriers beyond your control. There is no minimum income for most spousal sponsorships. You sign an undertaking to support your partner financially for three years.

Applicants living with their sponsor in Canada can apply in the spouse or common-law partner in Canada class and may be eligible for an open work permit while they wait. IRCC has said it expects waits in that class, outside Quebec, to move toward about 12 months under the 2027 measures.

Dependent children

Children under 22 who are not married or in a common-law relationship, and older children who have depended on their parents since before 22 because of a physical or mental condition.

Parents and grandparents

The Parents and Grandparents Program takes a limited number of applications, by invitation, and the sponsor must meet a minimum income for the three preceding tax years. The undertaking lasts 20 years. Because intake is limited, many families use the super visa, a multiple-entry visa that lets parents and grandparents stay for up to five years at a time. It requires a host who meets an income minimum and private medical insurance.

Other relatives

Sponsoring a brother, sister, aunt, uncle or other relative is possible only in narrow situations, such as an orphaned relative under 18, or when the sponsor has no closer family in Canada or to sponsor.

Common reasons applications stall

  • Thin relationship evidence. Officers look for a consistent story: shared finances, travel, communication, photos over time, and people who know you as a couple.
  • Missing police certificates or expired medical exams.
  • Not declaring every family member. An undeclared, unexamined family member generally cannot be sponsored later.
  • Quebec residents missing the additional provincial undertaking step.
