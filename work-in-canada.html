Work in Canada

Most foreign nationals need a work permit. Which one depends on whether an employer has to prove it could not hire locally, and whether the permit ties you to that employer.

Two programs, two logics

Temporary Foreign Worker Program (needs an LMIA)

The employer applies for a Labour Market Impact Assessment showing that hiring a foreign worker will not harm the Canadian labour market. With a positive LMIA and a job offer, you apply for an employer-specific work permit. The employer pays the LMIA fee; charging it to the worker is not allowed. The Global Talent Stream offers faster processing for certain technology and specialised roles.

International Mobility Program (LMIA-exempt)

These permits exist because the work brings a broader benefit to Canada or falls under an agreement. Examples:

  • Intra-company transferees moving to a Canadian branch of their employer.
  • Professionals covered by trade agreements such as CUSMA, CETA and CPTPP.
  • Post-graduation work permits for eligible graduates.
  • Bridging open work permits for people waiting on a permanent residence decision.
  • International Experience Canada.

Open or employer-specific

An employer-specific permit names the employer, location and sometimes the occupation; changing jobs means a new permit. An open permit lets you work for almost any employer. Open permits go to specific groups, including post-graduation applicants, some spouses and partners of skilled workers and students, and sponsored partners applying from inside Canada. Eligibility for spouses has been narrowed in recent years, so check the current rule for your partner's occupation or program.

International Experience Canada

Citizens of countries with a youth mobility agreement, usually aged 18 to 35 (18 to 30 for some countries), can apply in three categories: Working Holiday (an open permit), Young Professionals and International Co-op. Candidates enter a pool and are invited in rounds while places last for their country.

How work leads to permanent residence

  • One year of skilled work in Canada makes you eligible for the Canadian Experience Class and earns 35 to 40 CRS points, or more with further years.
  • High-wage occupations earn a bonus of 20, 40 or 70 points for 12 months of experience since 3 November 2026.
  • A valid job offer in a high-wage occupation earns 50, 70 or 100 points and no longer requires an LMIA to count.
  • Provincial streams for workers already employed in the province are often the most direct route for TEER 4 and 5 occupations that Express Entry does not cover.

Job offers and scams

A real Canadian employer does not charge you for a job offer or an LMIA, and does not ask you to pay through gift cards, cryptocurrency or a personal account. Verify the business independently and read our fraud guide before sending money or documents to anyone.
