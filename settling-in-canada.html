Settling in Canada

Approval is the midpoint. This checklist covers what to do before you fly and in your first 90 days so that work, health care and credit start on time.

Reviewed 12 November 2026

Before you leave

  • Gather originals: passports, confirmation of permanent residence or permit approval letter, birth and marriage certificates, degrees and transcripts, vaccination records, driving record and insurance history.
  • Have education and professional credentials assessed, and contact the regulator if your occupation is licensed in Canada (nursing, engineering, teaching, trades and many others are regulated by province).
  • Book temporary housing for the first two to four weeks. Signing a long lease unseen is the most common expensive mistake.
  • Use free pre-arrival services funded by IRCC for approved permanent residents.
  • Check what you can bring. Declare cash or instruments worth $10,000 or more at the border.

Week one

  • Social Insurance Number (SIN). Apply at a Service Canada office or online. You need it to work and to file taxes. Never share it casually.
  • Bank account. Major banks have newcomer packages with no monthly fee for a period and a first credit card without Canadian credit history.
  • Phone plan. A Canadian number is needed for almost every other sign-up.
  • Health card. Apply to the provincial plan immediately. Some provinces have a waiting period of up to three months, so buy private insurance to cover the gap.

First month

  • Long-term housing. Expect to provide first and, in some provinces, last month's rent. Learn the tenancy rules for your province; they differ on deposits and rent increases.
  • Settlement agency. Free services include job-search help, language classes (LINC in English, CLIC in French), school registration support and help with forms.
  • Children's school. Public school is free for children of permanent residents and of most work and study permit holders.
  • Driver's licence. Many provinces exchange licences from certain countries; others credit your experience toward testing. Bring an official driving record.
  • Family doctor. Join the provincial waitlist early; walk-in clinics and virtual care fill the gap.

First 90 days

  • Build credit. Use a credit card for small purchases and pay the full balance each month. Credit history affects renting, mortgages and phone contracts.
  • Canadian-style resume. One to two pages, no photo, age or marital status, results-focused.
  • Benefits. Families may qualify for the Canada Child Benefit; apply as soon as you have a SIN.
  • Taxes. You file a return each spring for the previous calendar year, usually by 30 April, even with little income. Filing is how you receive benefits and credits.
  • Permanent resident card. It is mailed to your Canadian address after landing. Update IRCC if you move.

Keep your status safe

Permanent residents must be physically in Canada for at least 730 days in every five-year period. Keep travel records; they also feed your citizenship calculation, which needs 1,095 days.
